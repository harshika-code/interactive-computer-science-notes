Unit 1: Blockchain Foundations

1. Centralized, decentralized and distributed systems

Centralized and decentralized describe who controls the system. Distributed describes how work and data are spread over several computers. The three are independent: a system can be distributed but centrally controlled.
CentralizedOne server holds everything.
All nodes depend on the centre.
Example: bank database, Gmail
DecentralizedSeveral independent authorities share control.
Example: consortium of banks
DistributedWork spread over networked computers.
Example: Google Search, Hadoop
Google Search is distributed but centralized in control. Bitcoin is both distributed and decentralized.
Quick check
Why does this matter?

Exam questions often ask you to classify a system and justify it. Use the four steps: who decides, where the data is, whether work is networked, and what happens when one machine fails.

3. Block structure and tamper detection (interactive)

Each block stores its own data and the hash of the previous block. Change any block's data below and watch the chain break. The hash function here is a toy hash for teaching, not SHA-256, but the linking logic is the same.

Changing one block breaks the link from the next block onward. To hide the change, an attacker must recompute every later block, which costs work. This is why an altered history is detectable and expensive to rewrite.

4. Hashing and the avalanche effect

Hash function: any input in, fixed-size digest out. Deterministic, fast, pre-image resistant, collision resistant, and shows the avalanche effect. SHA-256 and Keccak-256 give 256-bit (32-byte) outputs.

5. Merkle trees (interactive)

Four transactions are hashed in pairs up to one Merkle root. Edit any transaction. The root changes, which is how tampering shows up.

Verification trick: rebuild the tree from the data and compare roots. Matching roots mean the whole data set matches, without checking every transaction. Four transactions give 2 levels above the leaves.

7. Proof of Work and mining (simulator)

PoW security comes from the cost of computation. Its drawbacks are high energy use, expensive hardware, slow throughput, and possible concentration of mining power.

8. PBFT and fault tolerance (interactive)

PBFT needs n ≥ 3f + 1 nodes to tolerate f faulty ones. With 4 nodes, f = 1. Set the number of faulty nodes and see whether the honest nodes still agree.

Faulty nodes: 0

Worked check: n = 4 gives f = ⌊(4 − 1)/3⌋ = 1. With 2 faulty nodes out of 4, the rule is broken, so safety is not guaranteed.

Unit 2: Bitcoin

11. Bitcoin basics, supply and halving

Bitcoin (BTC): first cryptocurrency, proposed in a 2008 whitepaper by Satoshi Nakamoto, live from 3 January 2009 (Genesis Block). Peer-to-peer electronic cash with no bank or government middleman.
Halving calculator (210,000 blocks per era)
Era (0 = 2009 launch): 0

Total supply converges to 21,000,000 BTC: 210,000 × 50 × (1 + ½ + ¼ + …) = 210,000 × 50 × 2. Units: 1 BTC = 100,000,000 satoshis; 1 mBTC = 0.001 BTC; 1 µBTC = 0.000001 BTC.

Acquiring BTC

Centralized exchanges (KYC, custodial) · P2P trading (escrow, less identity disclosure) · Bitcoin ATMs · Mining · OTC desks for large trades. Custodial wallets hold your keys for you; non-custodial wallets leave key control with you ("not your keys, not your coins").

14. Bitcoin Script: P2PKH stack trace

Bitcoin Script is stack-based and not Turing-complete (no loops). The ScriptSig runs first, then the ScriptPubKey, on one stack. Step through the trace and watch the stack change.

If OP_EQUALVERIFY or OP_CHECKSIG fails, the input is rejected immediately. The input is valid only if one TRUE value is left on the stack.

22. Zero-knowledge proofs (cave simulator)

Ali Baba's cave. Peggy wants to prove she knows the magic word without saying it. Victor picks a path to see her come out of. A cheater guesses right with 50% chance per round.

Rounds: 20

Propertyzk-SNARKzk-STARK
Proof sizeSmall (~200 bytes)Larger (~50–200 KB)
SetupSome need trusted setupTransparent
BasisElliptic curveHash-based (quantum-resistant)
ExamplesZcash, zkSyncStarknet
Three properties: completeness (true statements are accepted), soundness (false ones are not), zero-knowledge (nothing beyond truth is learned). Blockchains need non-interactive proofs, which SNARKs and STARKs are.

A DID only proves control of a key. It carries no attributes. SSI is the goal, and DIDs are the identifier layer that makes it possible. Verifiers do not call the issuer live for each check, but they do read the issuer's DID and revocation data.

28. Green and sustainable blockchain

Energy use mainly comes from consensus (mining), running nodes, storage and communication, transaction execution, and cooling. Measure total annual electricity, carbon emissions, carbon intensity of the power source, hardware lifecycle and e-waste, and throughput, not just energy per transaction.

Efficient consensusPoS or permissioned instead of PoW
Layer 2Off-chain processing
RenewablesSolar, wind, hydro

Blockchain can record a carbon credit's history, but it cannot verify that the underlying physical claim is true.
